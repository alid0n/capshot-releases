Details that bite

Things that usually go wrong here

Screen recorders fail in specific, boring ways. These are handled.

Clips that won't play

Stopping a recorder by killing the encoder leaves a file with no index — right size, plays nowhere. Capshot asks the encoder to finish and waits for it.

Boxes of any shape

H.264 refuses odd pixel dimensions and writes an empty file rather than complain. Drag 731 × 419 and the readout tells you it's recording 730 × 418.

Your second monitor

A display to the left of your main one lives at negative coordinates. Tested against a real one sitting at -1920, -555.

GIFs that aren't enormous

Default settings elsewhere cost about 1.2 MB per second. Capshot builds a proper colour palette and caps the size, so a short clip stays shareable.

Why ChromeOS is different

ChromeOS doesn't let an installed app see the rest of the screen — a Linux app in the container would launch happily and record a black rectangle. So the Chromebook version is a web app, which can record and save to your folder, but can't sit in your shelf waiting or claim a keyboard shortcut across the whole system. Those two are real losses and it's better to say so here than to let you find out after downloading.
